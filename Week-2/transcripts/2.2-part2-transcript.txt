This standard is composed of a WIA that results in a well-being indicators dashboard for use by AIS creators in designing, developing, deploying, monitoring, and iteratively improving AIS that helps safeguard or improve human well-being. IEEE standard 710 is an iterative process that encompasses adaptation and continual improvement. When it is adopted, the WIA shall be conducted as an iterative process at every phase, from conception, design, and development to the ongoing assessment and management of the AIS. The WIA is composed of five activities, internal analysis and stakeholder user engagement, the creation of a well-being indicators dashboard, the creation of a data collection process, and the collection of data, and data analysis and the use of said data to improve the AIS in question and iteration.
The first activity, task one, internal analysis asks what is the AIS? What are the needs it meets and problems it solves? Who are the intended and unintended users and stakeholders, the impacts on human well-being, probability, and how are the negative impacts on human well-being considered and mitigated? They start with task number one and internal analysis. What is the AIS? The AIS service is the transportation system designed to provide the people of Finland with an integrated transportation network that uses a variety of AI AVs, and non-AI such as bike share systems in order to eliminate the need for private car ownership. The next step in the WIA task analysis asks, who are the intended users, the unintended users, and who are the stakeholders? Who the stakeholders are depends on what standard or framework you're using and how that standard or framework defines the stakeholders. So in this particular standard, we are looking at the list of definitions for intended and unintended user and stakeholders in order to complete this task. For this study, they first want to determine who the intended users of the AIS are. An intended user is defined as a person by whom the AIS creator intends the AIS to be used. In our example, the intended users, of course, are those who are giving up their personal vehicles, the general residents who are participating in the study. The project wants to be sure to include elderly residents and disabled residents who can greatly benefit from the project.
The team comes up with this list of intended users. To determine who the unintended users are, the team needs to do a little brainstorming. An unintended user is defined as a person who an AIS creator does not intend the AIS to be used, but who nonetheless will interact with the AIS system. After some brainstorming, the team comes up with this list, the children of residents, other family members who do not have a license to drive, tourists and guests of residents, people with suspended licenses, non-residents who are curious to try out the AI vehicles. A stakeholder is anyone or organization that is meaningfully or potentially meaningfully impacted by the AIS or meaningfully or potentially meaningfully impacts the AIS. The team brainstorms for a while and they come up with this list. Bus and for-hire ride-share drivers, the delivery service providers, and the AI algorithm data labelers, developers of the AI system, the insurance industry, pedestrians, public transit users, police, car dealerships, and road maintenance agency.
