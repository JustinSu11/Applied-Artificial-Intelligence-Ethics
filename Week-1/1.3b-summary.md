# 1.3b Publications and Communications of AI Ethics
*Read time: 2 min*

**One line:** AI governance runs on public documents
meant to shape behavior. They form a ladder, and each
word means something different.

*(You won't read most of these as a dev. Just know the
map.)*

## The ladder (soft -> concrete)

| Step         | Meaning                                    |
|--------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Value        | What's desirable. The moral compass.       |
| Principle    | High-level goal, free of context           |
| Framework    | Blueprint: how values and principles relate |
| Guidance     | How-to for applying a framework            |
| Requirements | Concrete, measurable "must do / must not"  |
| Assessment list | Checklist to verify the requirements   |

**Hook:** Compass -> Goal -> Blueprint -> How-to ->
Must-dos -> Checklist

**Note:** *guidance* is not the same as *guidelines*.

## EU example
- **Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI:** the
  framework (principles rooted in human rights:
  dignity, freedom, democracy, justice, equality,
  citizen rights).
- **ALTAI (Assessment List):** the checklist version.
- **EU AI Act:** binding law that grew out of it.
  Soft rules that hardened.

## The hard-rules ladder
- **Law:** rule from government that orders society.
- **Regulation:** detailed instructions for *carrying
  out* a law.
- **Technical standard:** accepted spec or protocol
  for an industry (like a unit of measure).

**Hook:** Law = what. Regulation = how. Standard =
exact spec.

## Who values what
- **Governments:** human rights, democracy.
- **Companies:** profit, innovation, efficiency.
- **EU principles:** human autonomy, prevention of
  harm, fairness.
- **OECD / GPAI:** inclusive growth, sustainable
  development, well-being come first.

**Questions to ask:**
- Is well-being the same as human rights?
- Is sustainable development the same as protecting
  the environment?
- "Inclusive growth": who is included?
