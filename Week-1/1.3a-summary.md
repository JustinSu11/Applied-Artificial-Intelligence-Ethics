# 1.3a Values and Intent of AI Ethics
*Read time: 2 min*

**One line:** Ethics says what AI *should* do. Governance
makes it happen. And rule-writers have their own agendas.

## Ethics vs governance
- **Ethics:** "What should AI do?"
- **Governance:** "How do we ensure it does?" Rules,
  processes, ideally enforceable.

## Two streams of governance
- **Technical:** build accountability into the system
  (design justification, testing, ethics by design).
- **Non-technical:** regulation, codes of conduct,
  certification, education, stakeholder input.

## Three levels
1. **Society:** national and international law, human
   rights (e.g., Universal Declaration of Human Rights).
2. **Institutions:** risk management, impact
   assessments, audits, due diligence.
3. **Individuals:** professional codes (ACM, IEEE, ISO)
   and design methods (privacy by design, ethics by
   design).

**Hook:** Big (laws) -> Medium (company process) ->
Small (your own work).

## Actors vs stakeholders (NIST)
- **AI actors:** play an active role in the AI lifecycle
  (build, deploy, operate).
- **Stakeholders:** everyone involved *or affected*.
- Actors are a subset of stakeholders.

## Who the actors are
Private sector - governments - international orgs -
multi-stakeholder groups - civil society - individuals.

- **Partnership on AI:** Apple, Amazon, DeepMind,
  Google, Facebook, IBM, Microsoft. Also runs the AI
  Incident Database.

## The big warning
Ethics and governance are **not value neutral.**
Documents reflect their author's interests, and may not
serve the public good. Always ask: **who wrote this, and
what do they gain?**

## 30-second recall
Ethics = goal. Governance = referee. Check the author.
