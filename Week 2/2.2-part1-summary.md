## Key definitions
- **Technical standard:** an official rule, accepted
  protocol, or way of operating. Provides specifications,
  guidelines, or requirements.
- **Taxonomy:** a **closed list of acceptable terms
  arranged hierarchically**, used to describe and
  classify content.
  - Not every standard or framework has one.
  - **Glossaries, definitions and terms** also provide
    classification.

## AI Impact Assessment (AI-IA)
- **Purpose:** identify **positive and negative impacts
  early**, to safeguard AI's benefits and avoid its
  downsides.
- There are **different views** of what an AI-IA is.
- **Form:** tools can be **self-assessments**, used for
  audits and for meeting legal or other requirements
  and standards.
- **Issue categories it covers:** human rights, ethics,
  data protection, privacy, security, safety,
  environmental impacts.
- Can be **embedded in organizational processes and
  structures** in different ways.
- Can be **part of a broader governance system** that
  supports responsible AI governance.

## Warning: not neutral
- **AI-IAs, ethics impact assessments (EIAs) and
  standards are not neutral.** Treat them with
  **discernment.**
- Their **implicit and explicit taxonomies operationalize
  certain values**, and so **often miss the true harms.**
- Example: a taxonomy can define **who is at risk** and
  **how harms are categorized**, which lets it
  **prioritize certain stakeholders' risks and harms**
  over others.

## The scenario: who is involved
- **INVIDA:** AI service provider (the AI developers).
  *(Spelled "NVIDA" in Part 2.)*
- **Government of Finland:** the client.
- **Cargle, Inc.:** AV car manufacturer.
- **SimTech:** smart city infrastructure partner.
- Adapted from **IEEE 7010 Annex E.**
- Based on **hypotheticals and two facts:**
  1. **Helsinki has adopted a goal to end personal car
     ownership.**
  2. **World Happiness Report (WHR):** Finland rated
     happiest country (2020: third time in a row; the
     transcript also says 2018 through 2022).

## Project goals and pilot
- **Goal:** transportation system with **no personal
  vehicle ownership by 2040.**
- City asks developers to include **"satisfaction with
  life"** as an indicator (the one used by the WHR).
- **Pilot:** on an **island community that approved the
  plan by a vote.**
  - ~**80%** of residents already rely fully on public
    transit.
  - ~**5,000** people are changing habits.
  - Lasts **one month**: the 5,000 car owners use no
    personal cars on the island.

## The AIS (AI system)
- An **integrated transportation network** mixing **AI
  AVs and non-AI options** (e.g., **bike share**) to
  **eliminate the need for private car ownership.**
- Must **compete with private car ownership on cost and
  convenience.**

**Design factors considered:**
- **Zero greenhouse gas emissions**
- **Accessible to people with disabilities**
- **Sensor systems for increased safety**
- **Technology that protects user privacy**
- **Explainable and interpretable communication
  interfaces**
- **Social interaction features:** AVs with **"faces"**:
  **lights = eyes, bumper = mouth.** The lights **pick up
  expressions from pedestrian faces** and **display the
  vehicle's intent.**

**Rider options:**
- Choose the **nature of the ride.**
- **Log personal preferences** (music, talk shows,
  entertainment) when sharing rides.
- Spend commute time **learning new skills, relaxing, or
  other activities.** **The AV encourages them to learn
  new skills.**

## Need it meets / problem it solves
- Fulfill **the city's goal** and give people **better
  urban transportation than personal vehicles.**
- **AV sensors pick up expressions and body movements**
  (faces, movements, behaviors) from **pedestrians.**
  This gives **feedback on non-riders, pedestrians, and
  bike traffic.**

## Data the team plans to use
- **Population, health, employment, educational** data.
- **Social media, gaming, streaming** data.
- **Future:** link **smart home sensors and health
  networks** to assess **family and individual
  relationships** (essential for human well-being).

## Ethics flags to watch (from the Canvas summary)
Not stated as such in the transcript; these are the

- **Societal good vs. individual rights.**
- **Non-neutral taxonomies** that may miss harms.
