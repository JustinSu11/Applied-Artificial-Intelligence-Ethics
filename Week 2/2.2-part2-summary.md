## IEEE 7010 and the WIA
- **IEEE 7010:** *Recommended Practice for Assessing the
  Impact of Autonomous and Intelligent Systems on Human
  Well-Being.*
- **WIA = well-being impact assessment.**
- Result: a **well-being indicators dashboard** for AIS
  creators to use in **designing, developing, deploying,
  monitoring, and iteratively improving** the AIS, to
  **safeguard or improve human well-being.**
- It is **iterative**: includes **adaptation and
  continual improvement.** When adopted, the WIA is done
  **at every phase** (conception, design, development,
  and ongoing assessment and management).
- **AIS** = the AI system being assessed.

**The WIA has 5 activities** (as listed in the
transcript):
1. **Internal analysis**
2. **User and stakeholder engagement**
3. **Creation of a well-being indicators dashboard**
4. **Creation of a data collection process** and
   **collection of data**
5. **Data analysis and use of the data to improve the
   AIS, plus iteration**

## Task one: internal analysis asks 5 questions
1. **What is the AIS?**
2. **What needs does it meet and what problems does it
   solve?** (so developers align **success measures with
   project objectives**)
3. **Who are the intended and unintended users and
   stakeholders?**
4. **What are the impacts on human well-being**, and how
   probable are they?
5. **How are negative impacts considered and
   mitigated?**

## Q1: What is the AIS?
An **integrated transportation network** using AI AVs
and non-AI options (e.g., **bike share**) to **eliminate
the need for private car ownership.** Must compete on
**cost and convenience.**

## Q2: Needs and problems
Fulfill **the city's goal** and give people **better
urban transportation than personal vehicles.**

## Q3: Users and stakeholders
**Who counts as a stakeholder depends on the standard or
framework's definitions.** This standard's definitions:

| Term | IEEE 7010 definition |
|---|---|
| **Intended user** | A person by whom the AIS creator **intends the AIS to be used** |
| **Unintended user** | A person the creator **does not intend** to use the AIS, who **nonetheless interacts with it** |
| **Stakeholder** | Anyone or any organization **meaningfully (or potentially meaningfully) impacted by** the AIS **or that meaningfully impacts it** |

**Intended users:**
- Residents **giving up personal vehicles** (general
  residents in the study).
- Deliberately **includes elderly and disabled
  residents** (who can greatly benefit).

**Unintended users** (from brainstorming):
- **Children of residents**
- **Other family members without a driver's license**
- **Tourists and guests of residents**
- **People with suspended licenses**
- **Non-residents curious to try the AI vehicles**

**Stakeholders** (from brainstorming):
- **Bus and for-hire ride-share drivers**
- **Delivery service providers**
- **AI algorithm data labelers**
- **Developers of the AI system**
- **Insurance industry**
- **Pedestrians**
- **Public transit users**
- **Police**
- **Car dealerships**
- **Road maintenance agency**

## Q4: Impacts on well-being
- Developers **brainstorm using their own knowledge and
  research.**
- IEEE 7010 requires **at least one indicator for each
  well-being domain.**
- Indicators are chosen **for each domain, aligned with
  project needs.**
- Domains and indicators **reflect established data sets
  used by multiple world indices**, so they **align with
  the World Happiness Report** (which ranked Finland #1).
- **Why:** lets the team **demonstrate project success
  and areas to improve**, and gives stakeholders a way to
  **monitor and track using the same indicators and
  measurements.**

## Data sources
- **Intended and unintended users:** **embedded sensors
  and feedback mechanisms** in the IoT networking system,
  **user mobile app data**, and **AV user interfaces.**
- **Unintended users and stakeholders:** AV sensors pick
  up **expressions and body movements** from pedestrians'
  faces, movements and behaviors, giving feedback on
  **non-riders, pedestrians, and community members.**
- **Riders:** can log preferences (music, talk shows,
  entertainment) and spend time learning new skills,
  relaxing, etc. The **AV encourages them to learn new
  skills** and **collects feedback on various well-being
  indicators.**

## Q5: Prioritizing stakeholders (tiers)
Based on **risk to safety, sustainability, and
well-being:**
- **Tier 1:** users and stakeholders **directly
  impacted.**
- **Tier 2:** **elderly and vulnerable populations.**
- **Tier 3:** **indirect and future** users and
  stakeholders.

## Iteration and handoff
- **Continuous iteration, improvement, and monitoring**
  of population health and well-being is key to
  Helsinki's sustainability initiative and alignment with
  other global actors.
- **At the end of the pilot**, the AI contractors **hand
  off project data** to **internal stakeholders, data
  analysts, research groups, government agencies, and
  sustainability and human well-being global partners.**
