Our first principle in this module is explainability, and that is defined as a set of processes and methods that allow human users to comprehend and trust the results and output created by machine learning algorithms. Explainability focuses on ensuring stakeholders, whether they're developers, users, or impacted individuals, can understand the logic and processes behind an AI system's outputs. So this helps build trust in AI systems by demystifying their inner workings. It enables accountability by clarifying decision-making pathways and supports ethical practices by revealing potential biases or errors. As we all know, that complex models can often function as black boxes making explainability difficult.

The second principle in this module is interpretability, and this is the extent to which an artificial intelligence system's internal processes and decisions can be understood and explained in human terms. Interpretability focuses on the clarity of relationships between input data, model mechanisms and outputs, and enabling humans to discern how and why the system arrived at a specific result. So this helps users trust and effectively use AI systems by making their decision-making processes intelligible. And it also identifies potential errors, biases, or areas for improvement within the model. And while explainability often involves creating tools or methods to describe AI behavior post-hoc, interpretability emphasizes inherent clarity in the model's design and functioning.

And finally, our third and last principle that we'll be covering in this module is transparency. And transparency is described as the clarity and openness in how AI algorithms and systems operate and make decisions, including their purpose, data sources, and operational mechanics. Transparency ensures that all stakeholders have access to the necessary knowledge to understand how and why the system functions as it does. This helps promote informed decision-making by stakeholders. It encourages ethical AI development by reducing hidden risks or biases, and also strengthens public trust and regulatory compliance. Transparency may be undermined by complex or proprietary AI systems, intellectual property, or trade secrets. This makes it harder for stakeholders to interpret the provided information.
