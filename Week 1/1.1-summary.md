# 1.1 The Power and Promise of AI
*Read time: 2 min*

**One line:** AI can do huge good, but *how it's built
and used* decides whether it helps or harms.

## The promise (where AI helps)
- Automation and data analysis (faster, more precise)
- Personalization (shopping, streaming, learning)
- Creativity (art, music, design)
- Healthcare (early detection, personalized medicine)
- Environment (monitoring, disaster prediction)
- Education (adaptive, not one-size-fits-all)
- Economic growth

## The catch
- Good intentions are **not enough**. You need
  **foresight** (think ahead) and **oversight**
  (keep watching).
- **Garbage in, garbage out:** bad data = bad results.
- What looks good today can be misused tomorrow.

## Case: predictive policing
**What:** data used to predict where crime will happen
(or who will offend).

**What went wrong (4 breaches):**
1. **Biased data:** past policing was uneven, so the
   data is too.
2. **Confirmation bias:** more patrols -> more arrests
   -> "proof" the area is high crime.
3. **No transparency:** proprietary models can't be
   audited, so nobody is accountable.
4. **Unconscious bias:** builders never asked how the
   data was collected.

**The core problem: a vicious feedback loop.**
Predict crime here -> police go here -> arrests here
-> data says "crime here" -> repeat.

**Result:** over-policing, stigma, lost community trust.

## Key facts
- PredPol (now Geolitica): ~1/3 of US agencies used
  predictive software by 2017.
- Chicago model: 85% of top-risk scores were African
  American men, many with no record. Likely driven by
  **proxies** (zip code, school).
- 2020: 1,400 mathematicians boycotted ("scientific
  veneer for racism"). Chicago and LAPD ended their
  programs. Santa Cruz was the first US city to ban it.
- May 2023: EU Parliament voted to ban it. Still used
  in the US.
