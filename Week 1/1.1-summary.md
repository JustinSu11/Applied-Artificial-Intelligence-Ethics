# 1.1 The Power and Promise of AI
*Ethics · Week 1 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** AI can do huge good, but *how it's built
and used* decides whether it helps or harms. Good
intentions are not enough.

## The promise
AI is one of the most significant advances in modern
technology. It can revolutionize industries and
**societal structures.**

| Area | What AI does |
|---|---|
| **Automation** | Automates repetitive, routine tasks. Manufacturing robots work with **precision and consistency.** Raises efficiency and productivity |
| **Data analysis** | Processes data volumes far beyond human capability. Deeper insights in **healthcare, finance, climate science** for better-informed decisions. Gives businesses a competitive edge |
| **Personalization** | Tailors experiences to individual preferences: **online shopping recommendations, personalized content streaming, adaptive learning systems** |
| **Creativity** | Pushes human creativity in **art, music, design.** Helps generate new ideas and concepts |
| **Healthcare** | **Personalized medicine, early disease detection, robotic surgery,** more efficient patient data management ("only at the beginning stages") |
| **Environment** | Monitors environmental changes, **predicts natural disasters,** helps develop more sustainable practices |
| **Education** | **Adaptive learning environments,** more accessible, tailored to individual learning styles. Moves away from **one-size-fits-all** education |
| **Economic growth** | Optimizes processes and creates new opportunities |

**Sci-fi that became real:** communication between Earth
and space, **self-driving vehicles, smart homes, virtual
personal assistants** (every smartphone carries one).

## AI timeline
- **1942:** Alan Turing cracked **Enigma** (encrypted German
  communications), turning the tide of WWII. Arguably the
  **start point of AI.**
- **1964:** **ELIZA**, the first chatbot.
- **1969:** **Shakey the Robot.**
- **1995:** **ALICE** chatbot, built with **natural language
  processing** techniques (revolutionary then).
- **1997:** IBM's computer beat **Garry Kasparov**, reigning
  chess champion.
- **2008:** **Siri** (Apple).
- **2011:** IBM's question-answering system won
  **Jeopardy.**
- **Today:** exponential growth in **generative AI.**

## The catch: realizing the promise
- A creation is **neither good nor bad.** It depends on
  **how it is built and how it is used.**
- **Good intentions are not enough.** AI needs:
  - **Foresight** (think ahead)
  - **Oversight** (keep monitoring)
  - **Out-of-the-box thinking**
- What seems good today may be **expanded later and used
  for unethical reasons.**
- If risks are mitigated, the payoff is improved **quality
  of life.**
- **"Not all AI is created equal."** How AI is *created* is
  just as important, if not more so, than how it's used.
- **Garbage in, garbage out** (George Fuechsel): if your
  data is garbage, the outcome will be too.

## Case study: predictive policing
**Definition:** using **predictive analytics combined with
law enforcement** to identify potential criminal activity.
A **data-driven prediction of where crime may occur.**

**Consequences that arose:**
1. **Reinforcement of existing biases**
2. **Over-policing**
3. **Stigmatization of communities**
4. **Lack of transparency** on how model decisions arose
5. **Degraded community-police relationships** nationwide

### Timeline
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1930** | First **uniform police report.** Covered **400 cities in 43 states**, recorded activity of **over 20 million individuals.** First US dataset on police activity |
| **2008** | Mass recession -> **police budget cuts** ("same service, less money") |
| **2009** | First **predictive policing symposium.** Soon after, **LAPD received $3 million** to trial location-based policing |
| **2012** | **PredPol** created (now **Geolitica**). One of the main predictive models deployed across the US |
| **2017** | US **DOJ** reported **about one third of agencies** used predictive software |
| **2020** | **1,400 mathematicians** signed a letter boycotting predictive policing |
| **2020** | **Chicago's inspector general** investigated, program **scrapped** |
| **2020** | **LAPD ended** its predictive policing efforts |
| **2020** | **Santa Cruz** = first US city to officially **ban** it |
| **May 2023** | **European Parliament** voted to **ban** predictive policing in the EU. **US programs still in use** |

### The mathematicians' letter
> "Given the structural racism and brutality in U.S.
> policing, we do not believe that mathematicians should
> be collaborating with police departments in this
> manner. It is simply too easy to create a **scientific
> veneer for racism.**"

**What "scientific veneer for racism" means:** data from
the **1930 report era** (including **Jim Crow** era data,
systemic racism, disenfranchising of communities) likely
went into the models. The model then builds a **vicious
feedback loop**: *"if crime happened here in the past, it
will happen here in the future"* -> **over-policing.**
Builders didn't consider **how or when the data was
collected.**

### Chicago's model
- Designed to predict an **individual's risk of being an
  offender** (different from location-based models).
- **85% of people with the highest scores were African
  American men.**
- Many labeled high-risk had **no criminal record at all.**
- Likely cause: **proxy attributes** (same schools, zip
  code) used to label innocent men "high risk."
- Chicago's inspector general scrapped it in **2020.**

### The 4 ethical breaches
1. **Biased data:** models rely on **historical crime data.**
   If it reflects bias (e.g., over-policing), the model
   **perpetuates it.** An over-policed minority community
   has more crime data -> algorithm suggests **more policing
   there, regardless of actual crime rate.**
2. **Confirmation bias:** more police presence -> **more
   arrests for minor offenses** -> data feeds back and
   reinforces "high crime zone" -> **self-fulfilling
   prediction cycle.**
3. **Lack of transparency and accountability:** many
   systems are **proprietary**, so it's hard to assess
   algorithms for bias. Without a transparent look at the
   model and data, you can't hold it **accountable.**
4. **Unconscious bias:** influences **development,
   deployment, and outcomes** of the models.

**Result:** **erodes trust** between law enforcement and
communities, especially if residents feel targeted based
on **race or where they live.**
