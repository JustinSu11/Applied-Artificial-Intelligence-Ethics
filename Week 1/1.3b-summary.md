# 1.3b Publications and Communications of AI Ethics
*Ethics · Week 1 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** Governance is a **ladder of publications**,
from values at the top down to binding law and technical
standards. Terms sound interchangeable but each is a
distinct type with its own function.

## Why these exist
- Unlike code (internal, for project eyes only), AI
  governance is **outward-facing publications** meant to be
  **disseminated widely** and **set the agenda** across
  sectors.
- **One main purpose:** **shape the behavior of an
  audience.** Forms: **professional codes and standards**
  for development, or **broad principles and frameworks**
  that steer AI discourse and build **societal norms.**
- Terms **can mean different things in different
  contexts.** This section just grounds the course's usage.
- They **don't necessarily come from a government**; they
  come from any AI actor (see 1.3a).
- As a developer you **probably won't read these.** Know
  where approaches, practices, methods, and technical
  standards **originate** (core principles and frameworks).

## The ladder (progression)
**Value -> Principle -> Framework -> Guidance ->
Requirements -> (Assessment list) -> Law / Regulation /
Technical standard**

| Term | Definition | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Value** | A **conception of the desirable** that influences the **selection from available modes, means, and ends of action** | The **moral compass** |
| **Principle** | **High-level desirable goal or foundation for ethical conduct** reflecting values and morals. Formulated **free of any specific context** (that's why they're principles) | Big goal |
| **Framework** | **Structured, overarching system or model** outlining **key elements, core values, and related principles** that steer goals. A **conceptual structure/architecture** that shapes approach to a topic: **high-level roadmap** defining components and their relationships. **Foundation for standards, policies, guidance.** Normally used **in the absence of well-defined standards and laws** | The frame / blueprint |
| **Guidance** | **Practical recommendations on how to implement** principles, requirements, or criteria defined in a framework. Often a **companion to a framework**. **Different from "guidelines"** | The how-to |
| **Requirements** | **Concrete, measurable criteria derived from principles** saying what an AI system **must do or avoid** to align with them. Translate principles into **specific, actionable objectives** that can be **implemented, monitored, assessed.** Appear as **sub-points under principles**; speak to **designers, developers, deployers.** A guidance contains requirements | The checklist items |
| **ALTAI** (Assessment List for Trustworthy AI) | EU document that **accompanies the framework**: has the requirements, **operationalizes the principles**, a **checklist** for developers, operators, deployers | The audit sheet |
| **Law** | A **rule made by a government** used to **order how a society behaves** | |
| **Regulation** | **Official rule or act of controlling something** made by a government **or other authority**. **Detailed instructions on how laws are carried out** | Law's instruction manual |
| **Technical standard** | **Official rule / accepted protocol** (e.g., a **unit of measurement** or way of operating) used in a **particular industry** such as manufacturing or digital services. Gives **specifications, guidelines, or requirements** for products, services, systems | Industry's agreed spec |

## Worked example: EU HLEG
**EU High-Level Expert Group on AI:** *Ethics Guidelines for
Trustworthy AI.* One document shows the whole ladder:
- **Framework** = the high-level architecture. Its chapters
  are the **guidance / guidelines.**
- **Core principles** rooted in **fundamental rights.**
- **Requirements** = how it reaches them (human autonomy,
  fundamental rights).
- Next in the progression: **ALTAI.**
- Difference from most other frameworks: **laws were
  derived from this.** The **EU AI Act is binding**
  (risk assessments and requirements follow).

**Quote:** "AI systems should improve individual and
collective well-being... four ethical principles, rooted in
fundamental rights, which must be respected... specified as
ethical imperatives such that AI practitioners should always
strive to adhere to them. **Without imposing a hierarchy,**
we list the principles in the **order of appearance of the
fundamental rights in the EU Charter:**
1. **Respect for human dignity**
2. **Freedom of the individual**
3. **Respect for democracy, justice, and the rule of law**
4. **Equality, non-discrimination, and solidarity**
5. **Citizens' rights**"

*(The quote says "four principles" but lists these rights;
the four EU principles are in the next section.)*

## Same topic, different lists (value differences)
- **Governments:** value and enact laws that **protect
  fundamental human rights**, show respect for the **system
  of government and democratic values.**
- **Companies:** **profit-oriented**, so **pro-innovation and
  efficiency.**

| Source | Core principles listed |
|---|---|
| **EU (HLEG)** | **Respect for human autonomy, prevention of harm, fairness** (real HLEG also has explicability; not said in lecture) |
| **OECD / GPAI** (lecturer thinks now the same) | **#1 inclusive growth, sustainable development, and well-being** |

**Lecturer's questions to think about:**
- Is **human well-being** the same as **human rights**?
- Is **sustainable development** the same as **concern for
  the environment**?
- When they say **inclusive growth**, **who do they mean to
  include**?

## Quick-check list
- Why do governance publications exist (one main purpose)?
- Put in order: framework, value, requirements, principle,
  guidance.
- Define value, principle, framework, guidance, requirements.
- Guidance vs guidelines? (guidance is a distinct thing)
- What is ALTAI and what does it do?
- Law vs regulation vs technical standard.
- Which EU document is binding? (AI Act)
- EU vs OECD/GPAI: what's #1 on each? What 3 questions does
  the lecturer pose?
- Does a developer need to read these? (Probably not; know
  the origin.)
