# 1.3a Values and Intent of AI Ethics
*Ethics · Week 1 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** Ethics says what AI *should* do. Governance
makes it happen. And rule-writers have their own agendas.

## Ethics vs governance
| | Question | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **AI ethics** | "What should AI do?" | Values |
| **AI governance** | "How do we ensure AI does what it should?" | **Policy-driven**, should (typically) have **enforceable mechanisms** |

**AI governance** = the **rules and processes that
coordinate behavior.** Needed because developing and
deploying advanced AI involves **many organizations and
individuals with distinct goals and incentives.**

## Two streams of governance
| Stream | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical** | Systems and processes that make the **technology's own activity accountable and transparent** | **Justifying design choices**, making the system accessible, **ethical by design**, **testing and validation** |
| **Non-technical** | Everything around the tech | **Regulation, codes of conduct, standardization, certification, education and awareness** (foster an ethical mindset), **stakeholder participation, social dialogue** |

## Three levels of governance
*(Lecture lists "individual, institutional, society" but
walks through society first.)*

1. **Society:** **national and international policy and
   regulation.** Drive a lot of individual and
   organizational activity. Include **human rights and civil
   rights** safeguarded in international agreements, e.g.,
   the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights.**
2. **Institutions:** guidance for **organizations** to follow
   or adopt.
   - **Risk management**
   - **Impact assessments:** privacy / data protection,
     **technology, ethics, social, human rights**
   - Extend to **quality assurance, data governance, due
     diligence, corporate social responsibility (CSR),
     algorithmic audits**
3. **Individuals:** a fast-growing number of **AI ethics
   frameworks** from companies, governments, other
   organizations, plus:
   - **Professional guidance:** **ACM, BCS**
   - **Standards initiatives:** **ISO, IEEE** (standards that
     consider AI ethics)
   - **Development methodologies** adapted for ethics:
     **privacy by design**, or more broadly **ethics by
     design / ethics in design**

**Hook:** Big (laws) -> Medium (company process) ->
Small (your own work).

## Actors vs stakeholders (NIST definitions)
- **AI actors:** persons, groups, or organizations **acting
  within a system of interest.** They play an **active role
  in the AI system lifecycle**, including organizations and
  individuals that **deploy or operate AI.**
- **Stakeholders:** **all organizations and individuals
  involved in or affected by AI systems, directly or
  indirectly.**
- **AI actors are a subset of stakeholders.**
- You'll hear both words constantly in this course.

## Who the global AI actors are (6 types)
1. **Private sector**
2. **Government**
3. **International organizations**
4. **Multi-stakeholder organizations**
5. **Civil society**
6. **Natural persons**

**Role of each (lecture summary):**
| Actor | Role |
|---|---|
| **Natural persons** | **Use** and are **deeply impacted** by AI systems |
| **National governments** | **Protect rights; make laws, regulations, standards** |
| **Nonprofits** | Variety of roles **improving aspects of society** |
| **International orgs** | **Facilitate cooperation** between countries |
| **Corporations** | **Develop and deploy AI for profit** |
| **Multi-stakeholders** | **Alliances** that form **technical agreements to gain industry advantages** |

The lecture's slides list example organizations per type,
AI standards organizations, industry/professional bodies,
and community/advocacy orgs (reference only).

**Partnership on AI (PAI):** emerging global actor in
governance. Members: **Apple, Amazon, DeepMind, Google,
Facebook, IBM, Microsoft.** Also working on the **AI
Incident Database.**

## The big warning
AI ethics and governance are **not value neutral.**
- Many publications, communications, guidance, and
  frameworks **steer AI's direction to future-proof AI and
  its ethical/regulatory climate for the authors' own
  interests and business interests.**
- So AI ethical governance **may or may not represent the
  societal good**, depending on **which actor it comes from.**
- Actors often benefit **at the expense of human rights and
  democracy.**
- **Looking at the values and intent of the actors** (and
  their communications and publications) is very important.

**Ask:** who wrote this, and what do they gain?
