# LAYER 1: LEARN IT

## The big idea
AI isn't a cloud. It's **mines, factories, data centers, and
labelers.** Ethics = take responsibility, build rules, and
count the long-term cost.

**Hook:** **Foundation, Frame, Future.**
Responsibility = foundation, governance = the frame that holds
it up, sustainability = the future you're building for.

## 1) The three principles
| Principle | Plain meaning | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsibility** | ethical obligation of individuals, orgs, governments to act accountably in developing, deploying, using AI; foresee societal, environmental, economic impact; **prevent harm proactively** | the **ethical foundation / starting point** |
| **Governance** | systems, policies, frameworks that regulate AI; align with societal values, protect people, promote fairness, accountability, transparency | the **mechanism for enforcing** responsibility; turns ethics into **actionable policy** |
| **Sustainability** | develop and use AI so it **preserves environmental resources, minimizes ecological harm**, and lets future generations enjoy AI's benefits | the **long-term vision** |

## 2) The White House AI Bill of Rights (US)
- Full name: **Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights.**
- Issued **October 2022**, Biden administration, by the
  **White House Office of Science and Technology (Policy).**
- **NOT legally binding.** A framework for responsible practice.
- **5 principles** to protect the public from automated systems:

| # | Principle | Goal | Key asks |
|---|-----------|------|----------|
| 1 | **Safe and effective systems** | test so they don't harm | consult diverse communities and experts; pre-deployment testing; risk identification and mitigation; ongoing monitoring; follow domain standards |
| 2 | **Algorithmic discrimination protections** | prevent and mitigate bias | proactive equity assessments; representative data; protect against **proxies** for demographics; accessibility for disabilities; pre-deployment and ongoing disparity testing; clear organizational oversight |
| 3 | **Data privacy** | people control their data | ask permission; respect choices on collection, use, access, transfer, deletion; privacy-by-design safeguards; no designs that obscure choice or
default to privacy-invasive settings; consent brief, plain-language; fix hard-to-understand notices |
| 4 | **Notice and explanation** | transparency about automated decisions | plain-language docs; say a system is in use and who's responsible; **clear, timely, accessible explanations**; keep notice up to date; tell people when functionality changes; explain even when automation isn't the sole input |
| 5 | **Human alternatives, consideration, and fallback** | human help in high-risk cases | opt out for a human alternative where appropriate; timely human consideration and remedy; fallback / escalation if system fails, errors, or you want to appeal |

**Hook for the five:** **S**afe, **D**iscrimination, **P**rivacy,
**N**otice, **H**uman. "**S**ome **D**ogs **P**refer **N**aps
**H**ere."

## 3) The 2023 Executive Order (US)
- Biden signed it in **2023**: comprehensive strategy for AI
  regulation; focus **safety, national security, ethics.**
- Quote: AI holds "extraordinary potential for both promise and
  peril"; "highest urgency on governing the development and use
  of AI safely and responsibly."
- Key fact: **no comprehensive federal law** specifically
  regulating AI in the US. Relies on **guidelines, executive
  orders, sector-specific regulation, and the NIST AI Risk
  Management Framework.** *(Transcript: "nits" = NIST.)*
- *My addition, may be out of date: this order was later
  rescinded by a new administration. Verify before relying on
  it. For the quiz, use the lecture.*

## 4) The EU AI Act
**World's first comprehensive regulatory framework for AI.**
Goal: **harmonize rules** across the EU and set a **global
standard.** Sorts AI by **risk to health, safety, fundamental
rights** into **four tiers:**

| Tier | Rule | Example (lecture) |
|---|---|---|
| **Unacceptable risk** | **banned** outright | social scoring / social credit like in China |
| **High risk** | strict requirements: risk assessments, high transparency, compliance documentation | AI with significant impact on rights or safety |
| **Limited risk** | minimal requirements: **transparency notices** | AI chatbot must tell you it's AI |
| **Minimal risk** | no specific requirements beyond general EU law; **most AI** | AI in video games, spam filter |

Influence: **Canada** (Artificial Intelligence and Data Act)
and the **UK** are drafting frameworks inspired by it.

**Hook:** a **traffic light + a ban:** red-banned, orange-
strict, yellow-label it, green-go.

## 5) Sustainability case: the Amazon Echo
**Source:** Crawford and **"Doer"/"Jeweler"** (transcript
garbled; the study is Kate Crawford and Vladan Joler, *Anatomy
of an AI System*, my addition).
**Point:** "Alexa, turn on the lights" triggers a **vast
planetary network** of resource extraction, human labor, and
data processing. Three parts: **material resources, human labor,
data.**

### Part A: Earth (materials)
- Starts at the **Salar** (transcript: "sailor") in **southwest
  Bolivia**, the world's largest flat surface, a high plateau
  with a few meters of salt crust, rich in **lithium**: an
  estimated **50 to 70%** of the world's lithium reserves.
- Lithium = key for **lithium-ion batteries**; nickname
  **"gray gold."**
- Amounts: **Tesla Model 3 ≈ 138 pounds**; **smartphone ≈ 8
  grams.**
- **Echo** is wall-powered with a battery base; Amazon says
  opening/repairing it **voids the warranty** -> short lifespan
  -> becomes **waste.**
- Parts built from elements that took **billions of years** to
  form. Average **smartphone lifespan 4.7 years** (Consumer
  Technology Association) drives more extraction.
- "Deep time": we mine Earth's history for a **split second** of
  tech time.

### Part B: Human labor
- Components made in low-labor-cost countries.
- **Amnesty International:** cobalt mining (for 16 multinational
  brands), workers paid about **$1/day**, hazardous conditions,
  violence, extortion, intimidation; **children as young as 7.**
- Contrast: **Bezos ≈ $275 million per day** (first 5 months of
  2018, Bloomberg Billionaires Index). A child miner in the
  **Congo** would need **> 700,000 years** nonstop to earn one
  day of that.
  *(My check: $275M / $1 per day = 275M days = about 753,000
  years. ✔)*
- **End of life:** e-waste. **India, Ghana, China:** workers
  dismantle by hand, exposed to **lead and mercury**, use **open
  burning and acid baths**, polluting air, water, soil.

### Part C: Data
- Voice commands stored, profiles built, models trained.
- **Data centers** run **24/7**, need enormous **electricity**
  and vast **water** to cool; many still rely on
  **non-renewable** energy -> greenhouse gases.
- Training = **millions of processing hours.**
- **Amazon criticized** for lack of transparency on its actual
  **carbon footprint.**
- Raises privacy, security, resource-consumption questions.

## Big picture recap
- Responsibility -> governance -> sustainability: **ethics,
  enforcement, future.**
- US = principles (non-binding), executive order, **no federal
  AI law.** EU = **binding, risk-tiered.**
- The Echo shows AI = **extraction + labor + data + energy.**
- Opaque corporate footprints (Amazon) = governance gap.

## Definitions (lecturer's wording, condensed)
- **Responsibility:** "ethical obligation of individuals,
  organizations, and governments to act with accountability in
  the development, deployment and use of AI systems"; recognize
  potential **societal, environmental, and economic impacts**;
  proactive steps to prevent harm.
- **Governance:** "systems, policies and frameworks that
  regulate the development, deployment, and use of AI
  technologies"; ensures alignment with societal values, protect
  individuals, promote **fairness, accountability, transparency.**
- **Sustainability:** "development, deployment and use of AI
  technologies in a way that preserves environmental resources
  to minimize ecological harm and ... future generations can
  enjoy the benefits of AI."

  governance = **mechanism for enforcing responsibility**
  (structures and processes; ethical obligations -> actionable
  policies); sustainability = **long-term vision**.
