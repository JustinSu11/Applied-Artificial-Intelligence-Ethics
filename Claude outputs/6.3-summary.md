**One line:** Without strong laws, **developers** are the ones
who have to turn abstract ethics into **checklists, code,

# LAYER 1: LEARN IT

## The big idea
**Problem:** frameworks and guidance are **neither concrete nor
technical**, so a developer can't tell what to *do.*
**Fix:** build tools that link principles to **tangible system
requirements.** This is not just translation; it means
**research and development** of tools, techniques, technologies.

**Hook:** a law says "build it safe." A **standard** says
"use this bolt size." Developers grab the bolt size.

## 1) Governance is a progression
Governance can **result in laws and regulations.** The lecture
covers the other end: governance aimed at **individual AI
developers** in the absence of regulatory standards, "in code."

## 2) What the developer toolbox looks like
Methods, criteria, **checklists, metrics, process models, code
design patterns, software libraries, software assistance, data
sets**, plus **technical standards.**
(Labs in the course show "ethics code" techniques; SHAP is an
example, my addition.)

## 3) Three kinds of "ethics + design" (as lecturer listed)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ethics by design** | technical **integration of ethical reasoning capabilities into AI** |
| **Ethics in design** | **engineering techniques for evaluating** the ethical capabilities of AI systems |
| **Ethics for design** | **standards and certification processes** to ensure integrity of stakeholders across the AI lifecycle |

*(My addition: this trio is from Virginia Dignum's work. The
transcript is choppy on the third; the wording above is the
best reading.)*
Also named: **responsible AI, human-centered AI, ethically
aligned design.**

## 4) Technical standards
- **Why they matter:** networked, distributed systems must
  collaborate; data must be exchanged **safely, reliably,
  securely, exactly** using standard protocols.
- **A form of negotiation and agreement** between international
  actors and multinational industry; whoever **dominates
  standard-setting gets a business advantage.**
- **Practical:** give **common language**, **interoperability**,
  **efficiency** -> the "de facto form of AI ethics governance."
- For developers, standards are **task-based**: direct, detailed
  instructions for building AI with **interoperability, safety,
  transparency, security.**
- AI standardization is **still at the very beginning**; ethics-
  specific standards are emerging from various organizations.

### The catch
- Technically useful standards **may not be the right place for
  ethics.**
- Standards bodies span different **cultures, governments,
  languages, legal systems** -> putting AI ethics into standards
  raises **major human rights issues.**
- Standards are **not neutral**; they reflect the **values of
  the actors** who develop them (and patented technologies).
- **Risk:** developers seeking ethical guidance go to
  **technical standards**, which are the **least likely** to
  include **human rights and societal perspectives.**

**Trap:** "standard = safe and ethical." No: it can lock in the
values of whoever wrote it.

## 5) Stakeholder taxonomies
**Taxonomy** (from 2.2): a **closed list of terms arranged
hierarchically.** Here: used in development for
**interoperability, efficiency, safety**, and in **incident
reporting, risk assessment, mitigation.** They give a **shared
vocabulary** to name and handle ethical risks across the AI
lifecycle.

## 6) Where to look
- **AI Standards Hub / database** (lecturer unsure, "I think
  it's an ISO...").
  *(My addition, verify: the AI Standards Hub is run by the
  Alan Turing Institute with BSI and NPL, not ISO.)*
- **ISO** has been bringing together global experts to
  **translate established risk and management-system standards
  into the AI context** -> practical guidance that **mitigates
  risk while promoting innovation.**

## Big picture recap
- No strong law -> **developers fill the gap** with tools.
- Toolbox: checklists, metrics, libraries, data sets, standards.
- Standards give **interoperability** but are **not neutral**
  and weak on **human rights.**
- Taxonomies = shared vocabulary for incidents and risk.
