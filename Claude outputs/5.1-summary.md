# LAYER 1: LEARN IT

## The big idea
An AI decision should make sense to humans. Part 1 is about
*seeing into* the system. Part 2 is about *who is invisible*
around the system (data owners, labelers). Both are the same
theme: **hidden things hide harm.**

**Hook:** a restaurant. **Transparency** = open kitchen window.
**Interpretability** = the recipe is simple enough to read.
**Explainability** = the waiter explains the dish afterward.

## 1) Explainability
**Problem:** a model gives an answer and nobody knows why.
- A **set of processes and methods** so people can understand
  and **trust** what a machine learning model outputs.
- Audience = **stakeholders**: developers, users, impacted people.
- Usually **post-hoc**: tools that describe behavior *after*
  the model is built (Week 5.2 SHAP is one).
- Why it matters: builds trust, enables accountability, exposes
  bias and errors.

**Trap:** complex models are **black boxes**, so explainability
gets hard exactly when the model is most powerful.

## 2) Interpretability
**Problem:** can a human follow the model's own logic?
- How far a system's internal processes and decisions can be
  understood **in human terms**.
- Looks at the link between **input data -> model mechanism ->
  output**.
- Difference from explainability: interpretability is
  **inherent clarity in the model's design**. Explainability is
  usually **tools bolted on later**.

**Example (my addition):** a small decision tree is
interpretable. A deep neural net needs explainability tools.

## 3) Transparency
**Problem:** outsiders can't tell what the system does or why.
- Clarity and openness about how a system operates and decides:
  **purpose, data sources, operational mechanics**.
- Helps informed decisions, reduces hidden risks and bias,
  builds public trust, supports **regulatory compliance**.
- **Undermined by:** complex or proprietary systems,
  **intellectual property, trade secrets.**

## 4) Why the three matter
Together they make AI decisions **make sense to humans** and
promote **trust, fairness, accountability.**
But it's also about **how data is collected, who controls it,
whose voices are heard.** That leads to AI colonialism.

## 5) AI colonialism
**Problem it names:** AI power and data concentrate in a few
rich hands; others supply the raw material.
- Control over AI tech and data affects **autonomy, power,
  influence.**
- Result: **consolidation of power** with those who have the
  resources to build and deploy AI.
- Risk: **replicating or worsening** existing inequities.

**Hook:** old colonies shipped out cotton and gold. AI
colonies ship out **data and labeling hours.**

### The 5 parallels to historical colonialism
| # | Parallel | Plain meaning |
|---|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Resource extraction | Data taken from poorer countries with lenient privacy laws, no fair pay. "Data is the new oil." |
| 2 | Economic exploitation | Data providers and pre-processing workers underpaid; the systems don't benefit them. |
| 3 | Cultural imperialism | Local languages and customs undervalued; Western English-speaking norms baked into global models. |
| 4 | Inequality and marginalization | AI's health, education, economic gains go to the connected and wealthy. |
| 5 | Dependency | Developing countries rely on foreign AI; hurts technical sovereignty and local innovation. |

## 6) The hidden labor behind "automation"
**Problem:** AI looks autonomous, but humans label the data.
- Source: **Jones**, book *Work Without the Worker*
  (micro-work). *(Transcript: "still, Jones"; author is
  Phil Jones, my addition.)*
- **Facial recognition** needs workers clicking face parts:
  label eyes, nose, mouth, tag the emotion.
- **Autonomous vehicles** need micro-workers labeling traffic
  and urban data. They can't learn just by absorbing data.
- Quote (lecture): "In the hour it takes Jeff Bezos to make
  13 million, a refugee earns mere cents teaching his
  algorithms to spot a car."

## 7) Case study: Oskarina Fuentes Anaya and Appen
Source: **MIT Technology Review** series on *AI Colonialism*.

**Story in order**
1. Signed up for **Appen** (one of about a dozen data labeling
   companies) as a **side gig** while a college student in
   **Venezuela**.
2. Economy tanked, inflation skyrocketed -> side gig became
   **full-time job.**
3. Later left Venezuela for **Colombia** (one of millions of
   Venezuelan migrants).
4. Shares her story **by name** despite **threats of
   retaliation from Appen.**

**How the system hurts workers**
- Tasks are **first-come, first-served** -> must be always
  available. She sat at the computer **18+ hours a day** and set
  **loud alarms** at night.
- Instructions often **vague or missing.**
- Wrongly done task -> **rejected, unpaid** (even after hours).
- Accounts can be **suspended**, freezing withdrawals.
- Slow weeks: she earns **$7-$8**; minimum to withdraw is
  **$10**, so she can't take the money out.
- Opaque algorithms control earnings and schedule.
- Still depends on Appen; hopes platforms address **health,
  security, financial needs.**

**Bigger lessons**
- Labelers are the **backbone** of AI but **invisible**; this
  keeps the **myth of autonomous AI** alive.
- They're exposed to **war footage, sexual abuse, crime scenes**
  with **no mental health support, counseling, or debriefing.**

## 8) Why Venezuela?
"**Perfect storm**": economic crisis + high unemployment +
internet access + **well-educated population** = high-quality
work at **low cost.** Model then spread to **Kenya, North
Africa, parts of Asia.** Mirrors colonial resource and labor
extraction.

## Big picture recap
- **Explainability** (tools after), **interpretability**
  (clear by design), **transparency** (open about purpose,
  data, mechanics).
- Opaque systems hide errors, bias, discrimination -> less
  trust, harder compliance.
- AI colonialism = extraction of **data + labor** from the
  vulnerable, benefits flow to the powerful.
- "Autonomous" AI is propped up by **invisible human labor.**

## Definitions (lecturer's wording)
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Explainability** | "a set of processes and methods that allow human users to comprehend and trust the results and output created by machine learning algorithms." |
| **Interpretability** | "the extent to which an AI system's internal processes and decisions can be understood and explained in human terms." |
| **Transparency** | "the clarity and openness in how AI algorithms and systems operate and make decisions, including their purpose, data sources, and operational mechanics." |
| **AI colonialism** | occurs "when data collection and AI deployment disproportionately exploit or marginalize communities." A new form of dominance where control over AI tech and data affects autonomy, power, influence. |

## What each one does (lecturer's lists)
**Explainability:** builds trust by demystifying inner
workings; enables accountability by clarifying decision-making
pathways; supports ethical practices by revealing potential
biases or errors. Complex models often function as black boxes.

**Interpretability:** helps users trust and use AI by making
decision-making intelligible; identifies potential errors,
biases, areas for improvement. Focuses on clarity of
relationships between **input data, model mechanisms, outputs.**
Explainability = tools/methods to describe behavior **post-hoc**;
interpretability = **inherent clarity in design and function.**

**Transparency:** all stakeholders have access to necessary
knowledge; promotes informed decision-making; encourages
ethical AI development by reducing hidden risks or biases;
strengthens public trust and regulatory compliance.
**Undermined by:** complex or proprietary systems, IP, trade
secrets (harder to interpret info provided).

## Opaque (black box) risks (summary list)
Hide errors, biases, discriminatory logic; erode public trust;
undermine accountability; complicate regulatory compliance.

## AI colonialism: five parallels (lecturer's order)
1. Resource extraction (data is the new oil)
2. Economic exploitation
3. Cultural imperialism
4. Inequality and marginalization
5. Dependency

Details: wealthy entities exploit data of people in
**less developed countries with more lenient data privacy
laws**, **without fair compensation.** Data-preprocessing
workers not fairly paid; data not used to build systems that
benefit them. Local customs and languages undervalued in global
models; biases and norms of **Western English-speaking
developers.** Dependency = "slippery slope" that could undermine
**technical sovereignty** or encouragement of **local
innovation.**

## Micro-work and labeling
- Book: *Work Without the Worker* (Jones).
- AI relies on large data that must be **processed and
  labeled** first -> human labor.
- Examples: facial recognition (face-tagging, label eyes, nose,
  mouth, emotion); autonomous vehicles (label traffic loads,
  urban areas).
- Bezos quote: "In the hour it takes Jeff Bezos to make 13
  million, a refugee earns mere cents teaching his algorithms
  to spot a car."
