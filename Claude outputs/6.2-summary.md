# LAYER 1: LEARN IT

## The big idea
Different frameworks ask different questions. Utilitarian: "what
are the results?" Duty: "did you break a rule or right?"
Virtue: "what kind of person does this make you?" Others widen
the circle to community, long-term, nature. Strong ethics uses
**several lenses**, not one.

**Hook:** judging a movie: the **critic** (results), the **rule
book** (duty), the **character study** (virtue), the **family**
(community).

**Why now:** harms already happened, mostly from **non-malicious
intent + design flaws**: **loan rejection bias, medical
misdiagnosis.** Or **malicious misuse**: **psychometric voter
manipulation, facial recognition surveillance.**

## 1) The seven lenses
### Utilitarianism (a consequentialist theory)
- Right/wrong depends on **consequences.** Classic form: only
  **pain/pleasure, happiness/misery** count.
- **Objection:** it can justify clearly immoral acts. Example:
  Ivan challenges his brother Alyosha (*The Brothers Karamazov*)
  to accept torturing a baby to bring happiness to all humanity.
- **Utilitarian reply:** any theory that weighs welfare has the
  hard problem of measuring welfare.
- **Hook:** the **calculator**: biggest total happiness wins.

### Deontology (duty ethics)
- Focus: **respect, autonomy, rights**, never treating humans as
  **mere things or means.** Acts are right/wrong because of
  **what they are**, regardless of consequences.
- Example: **Kant** and never lying, even to save lives.
  *(Transcript says "exceptionalist"; likely "exceptionless".)*
- **Objection:** **rigid**, inflexible, ignores consequences in
  complex cases.
- **Hook:** the **rule book.**

### The organ-transplant test (both lenses)
Doctor with 5 patients needing organs; a healthy visitor walks
in. Killing the visitor to save five:
- **Duty ethics:** **wrong**, treats a person as a mere means.
- **Consequentialist:** might say **one death to save five**
  is the best outcome, **but** if caught, trust in healthcare
  collapses -> **worse long-term consequences.**
Lesson: frameworks give **different reasons**; agreement is
elusive.

### Virtue ethics
- Guidance from **character**: develop **virtues**, avoid
  **vices.** Most prominent Western version: **Aristotle.**
- **Objections:** **lack of clear guidance**, **cultural
  subjectivity** (virtues differ by culture), **hard to resolve
  dilemmas.**
- Aristotle: some things matter more than my life, like the
  **liberty of my city-state, safety of friends and family** ->
  worth risking death.
- **Hook:** the **role model.**

### African ethics and Ubuntu
- Source: **John Mbiti** *(transcript: "Muji", garbled; my
  addition)*; centered on **wellbeing**; societal wellbeing is
  **communal**; individual and group wellbeing have a place but
  **not at society's expense.**
- **Ubuntu:** "**I am because we are**" *(transcript scrambles
  as "we are, therefore I am")*.
- **Hook:** **the village.**

### Buddhist ethics
- Arose as a **therapeutic** response (not theoretical) to two
  questions: (1) **causes and conditions of suffering, conflict,
  trouble**; (2) **how to dissolve them.**
- See things as **impermanent, without fixed essence**; seeing
  otherwise causes **relational blockages and entanglements.**
- Mixes virtue, duty, consequence elements, but what matters is
  not just immediate consequences but **medium and long-term
  relational ramifications**; expands **spatial and temporal
  scope** of evaluation.
- **Hook:** **ripples in a pond.**

### Jewish ethics
- Based on **the Torah**, interpreted over thousands of years
  and across places; legal precepts **plus narratives** giving
  context; complex mix of legal and philosophical thought.
- **Value of human life is paramount**, outweighing nearly all
  other concerns; grounded (per lecturer) in humans being
  **created in the image of God**, with the unique power of
  speech.

### Indigenous ethics
- Hard to define: **don't treat Indigenous peoples as
  homogeneous** (pan-Indian view is problematic).
- Core: **interconnectedness of all things**: people, land,
  non-human animals, past and future generations, even spirits,
  rocks, rivers. Universe = **living entity** in constant
  transition; **balance and harmony.**
- No universal rules; key concepts: **harmony, reciprocity,
  gratitude, humility.**
- Source names (garbled): Joseph ... **Miller** and Andrea
  **Sullivan-Clark**.

## 2) Case study: "Selling Women Synthetically in India"
1. **Rana Ayyub** *(transcript: "Aab/Ayubb"; my addition)*:
   investigative journalist, member of India's **Muslim
   minority**, critic of **BJP** members.
2. **April 2018:** she wrote a report on the **rape of an
   8-year-old girl in Kashmir**; went on the **BBC** and called
   out BJP members marching in support of the accused.
3. **Blowback:** fake tweets in her name with anti-Indian views;
   then a **pornographic deepfake** circulated on **WhatsApp**
   (a BJP insider warned her).
4. **End of 2020 (per lecture):** other prominent Muslim women
   (journalists, activists, attorneys) were listed on a **spoof
   auction site**, named with a derogatory word for Muslim
   women; **doctored images**, sexually explicit or demeaning.
   *(My addition: public record often dates these sites to 2021
   and 2022. Use the lecture's date for the quiz.)*
5. Victims named: a **26-year-old Columbia University** student
   (**Hiba Beg**, transcript "Hepa Beg") who said it aims to
   force Muslim women who speak out to withdraw from public
   life; **Ismat Ara** (transcript "Ima Ara") tweeted a screenshot of her
   "auction".
6. **Delhi police** charged **two people**: **sexual harassment**
   and **promoting enmity between ethnic and religious groups**;
   accounts taken down; **real deterrent effect unclear.**

### The case through each lens
| Lens | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Utilitarian / consequentialism | serious harm to victims; misuse leads to disastrous consequences |
| Deontology | manipulating images **without consent** violates universal duties and individual rights |
| Virtue | perpetrators showed **cruelty, malice, recklessness** (vices); lack moral character |
| Buddhist | stresses **avoiding harm and fostering compassion**; **both absent** |
| Jewish | violates **human dignity, justice**, duty to stand against oppression |
| Indigenous | violates **relational accountability and interconnectedness** |
| African (Ubuntu) | violates **human dignity, community, justice**: "I am because we are" |

**Takeaway:** every framework **condemns** it, **for different
reasons**; together they give a holistic view.

## 3) From theory to practice: 3 approaches
| Approach | Idea | Details / example |
|---|---|---|
| **Principles-based** | define universal, abstract guidelines: fairness, transparency, privacy | **Montreal Declaration** for responsible AI *(transcript: "of a Syl")* |
| **Ethical by design** | embed ethics from the start | **co-design with stakeholders**; technical governance: **auditing, impact assessments, human-in-the-loop** |
| **Ethical consciousness** | build a **culture** of ethical awareness among developers, deployers, decision-makers | draws from **business ethics**; societal shift toward ethical AI use |

**Closing line:** ethical AI, theory or practice, needs a
**global, inclusive** approach (not only Western views).

## Big picture recap
- Frameworks = lenses: results, duty, character, community,
  long-term, human life, interconnection.
- Western three (**consequence, duty, virtue**) ≠ the whole
  world; add **Ubuntu, Buddhist, Jewish, Indigenous.**
- Deepfake case: all lenses condemn, for different reasons.
- Practice = **principles, by design, consciousness.**
