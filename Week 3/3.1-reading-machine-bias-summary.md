# 3.1 Reading: "Machine Bias" (ProPublica, 2016)
*AAI-531 · Week 3 recommended reading · paraphrased, not quoted*

> **Content note (from the course):** this article discusses
> racial bias, criminal justice disparities, and outdated
> terminology that may be sensitive to some readers. It is
> included to critically examine bias in AI and encourage
> discussion on fairness and ethical AI development.

**One line:** ProPublica tested the COMPAS risk score on
Broward County defendants and found its **errors fell unevenly
by race**, even though race isn't an input.

## Basics
- **Authors:** Angwin, Larson, Mattu, Kirchner. **Published
  May 23, 2016.**
- **COMPAS** is made by **Northpointe** (founded 1989; sold in
  2011 to Constellation Software). Used to score risk of
  reoffending.
- Its **137-question** survey does **not** ask about race.
  The formula is **proprietary.**

## The test
- **7,000+ people** arrested in **Broward County, Florida**
  (2013-2014).
- Followed for **2 years** to see who was charged again.

## Findings
- **Accuracy was weak:** about **61%** of those predicted to
  reoffend were arrested again. For **violent** crime only
  about **20%.**
- **Error pattern (the key result):**
  | | Black defendants | White defendants |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Did NOT reoffend but labeled higher risk** | about **45%** | about **24%** |
  | **DID reoffend but labeled low risk** | about **28%** | about **48%** |
- After controlling for prior record, age, and gender, Black
  defendants were about **77% more likely** to be flagged for
  future **violent** crime and **45%** for any future crime.
- Individual cases in the article (Borden vs Prater, Fugett,
  Parker, Zilly, Loomis) show the human side.

## Pushback
- **Northpointe disputed** the analysis and the method.
- Core tension: ProPublica looked at **error rates by group**
  (equalized-odds style). Others argue for **equal predictive
  accuracy per score.** Both can't hold at once when base rates
  differ. That's the **fairness-metric trade-off** from the lab.

## Link to the lecture
- Same case as the 3.1 lecture (**COMPAS**).
- Bias in a model can come from **historical data** even when
  the protected attribute is absent (**proxies**).
- Choosing a **fairness metric is a value decision.**
