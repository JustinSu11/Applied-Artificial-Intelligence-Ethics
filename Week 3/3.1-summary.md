# 3.1 Bias and Fairness (COMPAS)
*AAI-531 · Week 3 · cross-checked against the transcript*

**One line:** Bias is systematic unfairness baked into AI. Fairness
means no unjust discrimination. COMPAS is the case where biased
history produced biased risk scores, measured with **disparate
impact** and the **four-fifths rule.**

## Definitions (quote-ready)
**Bias (in AI ethics):** **systematic errors or unfairness** in AI
systems that arise when data or algorithms **reflect pre-existing
prejudices, inequalities, or assumptions.**
- Outcomes **favor certain groups over others**, perpetuating
  discrimination or unequal treatment based on **race, gender,
  socioeconomic status, or other protected attributes.**
- Bias may occur at **3 stages: data collection, model training,
  deployment.**
- Addressing it is critical so AI is **fair, transparent, and
  inclusive.**

**Fairness:** the principle that AI should **treat all individuals
and groups equitably without unjust discrimination or favoritism.**
- Decisions must **not disproportionately harm or benefit**
  particular groups based on race, gender, socioeconomic status, or
  other protected characteristics.
- Addresses **equal treatment:** don't disadvantage one group over
  another **without valid reason.**

**Two more fairness ideas:**
1. **Equitable outcomes:** results should be **fairly distributed**,
   giving everyone access to opportunities and resources, to avoid
   **systemic inequalities.**
2. **Transparency and accountability:** fair systems allow **clear
   understanding of how decisions are made**, so biases can be
   **identified and corrected.**

## Case: COMPAS
- **COMPAS = Correctional Offender Management Profiling for
  Alternative Sanctions.**
- A **risk assessment tool** used widely in the **US criminal
  justice system.**
- **Function:** predict the **likelihood a defendant will commit
  another crime (recidivism).**
- **Inputs:** data points such as **age, criminal history, and
  personal characteristics.**
- **Output:** a risk score of **low, medium, or high.**
- Outputs **directly influence bail, sentencing, and parole.**
- Algorithm is **proprietary**: hard for the public or even legal
  experts to scrutinize.

### What went wrong (ProPublica, 2016)
- **ProPublica's 2016 study** found significant **racial bias.**
- **Black defendants** were often **falsely labeled high risk.**
- **White defendants** were more likely to be labeled **low risk.**
- Major issue = **fairness**: individuals of different races were
  **treated differently.**

### Why: biased training data
- Models learn from **historical data.** If it reflects systemic
  bias (e.g., **over-policing of Black communities**, **higher
  arrest rates for minorities**), the model **learns and replicates**
  those patterns.
- Black defendants are historically more likely to be arrested or
  convicted due to **racial profiling**, not an inherent likelihood
  of reoffending.
- So the algorithm **wasn't neutral**; it **reinforced existing
  social inequities.**

### Equal treatment vs equal outcomes (the dilemma)
| Approach | Question |
|---|---|
| **Equal treatment** | Make the AI **equally accurate across groups**, so Black and white defendants face the **same error rates** and risk of false classification? |
| **Equal outcomes** | Focus on **real-world consequences** (incarceration rates, recidivism rates) being **equitable across groups**? |

- COMPAS appeared to favor **equal treatment** in some ways, but
  because of biased historical data, **outcomes were not fair.**

### The error pattern
| Group | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Black defendants** | **Far more likely falsely labeled high risk** (predicted to reoffend when they were in fact less likely to) |
| **White defendants** | **More often falsely labeled low risk** (judged less likely to reoffend, when they actually had a higher likelihood) |

- The lecture calls this **disparate impact**: prediction accuracy
  **differed across racial groups.**
- Result: unequal treatment = a **major fairness violation** in
  criminal justice.

## Disparate impact (the metric)
- Looks at **outcomes, not intent.** A system **not designed to
  discriminate** can still **disproportionately harm** groups.
- **Formula:**

  **DI = selection rate of the minority group / selection rate of
  the majority group**

- **Selection rate** = the **percentage of individuals in each group
  who receive favorable outcomes.** (COMPAS example: % of Black
  defendants classified **low risk** vs % of white defendants.)

### Legal link: the four-fifths rule
- **US law:** **Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964**:
  decisions (employment and others) that cause **disparate impact
  on protected groups** can be deemed **discriminatory.**
- Courts often apply the **four-fifths (80%) rule:**
  - **DI < 0.8 -> the minority group is significantly
    disadvantaged.**
- Widely applied in **employment and education**; **increasingly
  considered for AI** like COMPAS.
- Message: fairness is about **real-world impact**, not just how
  the model was designed.

## Conclusions from the lecture
- Bias in high-stakes areas like **criminal justice** has serious
  real-world consequences.
- Bias enters through **historical data** and is **perpetuated by
  algorithms.**
- COMPAS shows the challenge when **history reflects societal
  bias**, and that it **disproportionately affected minority
  groups.**
- Key needs: **regular auditing, transparency, accountability, and
  constant monitoring.**
- Ethical AI = **balancing innovation with responsibility**; an
  ongoing challenge needing **collaboration among all
  stakeholders.**
